## The Stress Test

After stabilizing the multi-lingual beauty taxonomy, I decided to validate a core hypothesis: Can the same search enhancement middleware scale across entirely different verticals without touching the engine's core code?

I picked Pet Care & Nutrition (Indonesia and Thailand) — a sector notorious for chaotic, hyper-colloquial search patterns.

The result? The system smoothly onboarded the new vertical in less than a day.

---

## What I Actually Changed

All I did was shift the inputs:
- Swapped the seed keyword dictionary for the data collection pipeline (from "serum" to "makanan kucing")
- Defined pet-specific IMEP rules (Species → Health Concerns → Active Ingredients)
- Turned on the Multi-Agent review pipeline to auto-classify incoming terms

---

## The Language Gap in Pet Care

The pet vertical has an even wider language gap than beauty.

Anxious pet owners search for a symptom: "kucing mencret" (cat diarrhea) or "bulu rontok" (hair loss). But merchants label products with a scientific solution: "Digestive Probiotics" or "Salmon Oil / Omega-3."

The native search bar sees zero overlap and throws a hard "Zero Results" page. IntentFlow bridges that gap by mapping colloquial panic-searches to standard product inventory.

---

## Why This Matters

This validation proves the engine is vertical-agnostic; the taxonomy layer is purely a decoupled configuration.

Because of this, expanding into 3C Electronics and Modest Fashion is already underway — using the same pipeline, at the same speed.

Commercially, it means the marginal cost of entering a new vertical approaches zero. No engineering overhead. No additional infrastructure. Just domain knowledge encoded as data assets.

For a solo founder, scalability isn't a feature. It's a survival mechanism.
